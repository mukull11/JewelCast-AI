# Inventory Optimization (M4)

This document describes the M4 inventory optimization engine: how demand
forecasts become inventory decisions, how the historical simulation works, and
which assumptions and limitations apply.

**Status:** implemented for the **synthetic Indian fashion-jewellery dataset only**.
UCI Online Retail II and M5 contain no inventory or purchase-order records, so the
engine refuses to invent inventory for them.

> Inventory optimization is a planning exercise on **simulated** data. It is not
> connected to Voylla or any real retailer's systems. No financial savings or
> real-world service improvements are claimed.

---

## 1. Business problem

A fashion-jewellery retailer must answer, for each SKU:

- How much stock is available now?
- How much demand is expected while waiting for a supplier?
- When should the SKU be reordered?
- How many units should be ordered?
- Which SKUs risk a stockout, and how urgently?
- Which SKUs hold excess stock?
- How do these decisions change if demand rises or falls, lead times grow, or the
  target service level changes?

M4 connects the M3 demand forecasts to these decisions and evaluates a
replenishment policy through a causal historical simulation.

---

## 2. Data sources

### Supported dataset

Only `data/processed/synthetic/` is supported:

| File | Rows (current build) | Fields used |
|---|---:|---|
| `daily_sales.parquet` | 175,165 | `date`, `sku`, `category`, `units_sold`, `available_stock`, `stockout_flag` |
| `inventory_snapshots.parquet` | 25,103 | `snapshot_date`, `sku`, `available_stock`, `incoming_po_qty`, `committed_qty` |
| `purchase_orders.parquet` | 10,952 | `po_id`, `sku`, `order_date`, `expected_arrival_date`, `quantity`, `status`, `supplier_lead_time_days` |
| `sku_master.parquet` | 300 | `sku`, `category`, `launch_date`, `supplier_lead_time_days` |

All of these records are generated by `src/data/synthetic.py` and are explicitly
simulated. The generator uses an `(s, S)`-style policy, lost sales when stock hits
zero, weekly snapshots and purchase orders with supplier lead times.

### Field semantics and alignment

- **`daily_sales.available_stock`** is start-of-day stock: orders due that day have
  already arrived and that day's sales have not yet been served.
- A recommendation at date *d* therefore uses stock at the start of *d* and a
  forecast whose **origin is d − 1**. Same-day sales are never used.
- **`inventory_snapshots.available_stock`** is end-of-day stock on Mondays. It is
  **not** used as the decision stock; the daily panel is used.
- **`purchase_orders`** are aligned as follows. A PO is pending at *d* when:
  - it is not cancelled/voided,
  - `order_date <= d`, and
  - `expected_arrival_date > d` **or** its recorded status is still `open`.

  Orders already due at *d* are already included in start-of-day stock. Pending
  orders are de-duplicated by `po_id` and summed once per SKU, so an incoming
  order is never double-counted.
- **`committed_qty`** exists only in weekly snapshots, and the generator derived it
  from the **following 7 days of demand**. Using it for a historical decision
  would leak future information. It is therefore excluded from decisions by default
  (`inventory.use_committed_stock: false`) and reported as context only.
- Invalid dates, non-positive quantities and cancelled orders are dropped and
  counted in metadata. Negative stock is clipped to zero. Missing stock is flagged
  as `stock_missing`, never silently presented as an ordinary zero.
- Duplicate daily rows, snapshots and purchase orders are de-duplicated before use.

### Unsupported datasets

`load_inventory_data` raises an error for `uci` and `m5`. Their `available_stock`
is null, not zero, and no purchase orders exist. Forecasting-only behaviour for
those modes is unchanged.

## 3. Forecast connection (M3)

The engine reuses the M3 global model and the same leakage-safe feature pipeline.
It never re-implements forecasting.

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| `ModelForecastProvider` | Fits a causal model at a forecast origin. `frozen` fits once and reuses; `rolling` refits every `refit_every_days`. |
| `SavedModelForecastProvider` | Uses a persisted M3 model **only if** its recorded training cutoff is not later than the forecast origin. |
| `ArtifactForecastProvider` | Reads persisted M3 forecast Parquet for a specific origin. |
| `TrailingMeanForecastProvider` | Causal trailing-average baseline used by the simple reorder-point policy. |

Rules:

- A forecast with origin *o* may use observations up to and including *o* only.
- Forecast dates used for inventory start at the decision date (*o + 1*).
- If a SKU has no forecast, a documented trailing-average fallback is used and the
  row is flagged `forecast_missing_fallback`. Missing forecasts are never replaced
  with actual future demand.
- Forecast horizons of 7, 15 and 30 days are produced by M3; the inventory engine
  uses `inventory.forecast_horizon` (default 30 days) so it can cover configured
  supplier lead times. The lead-time and protection-period calculations sum only
  the days they need.

---

## 4. Inventory position

```
Inventory Position = Available Stock + Confirmed Incoming Stock − Committed Stock
```

- **Available stock:** start-of-day on-hand units from the daily panel.
- **Confirmed incoming stock:** pending purchase orders that pass the alignment
  rules in Section 2. Only orders that genuinely exist are counted.
- **Committed stock:** represented in the synthetic snapshots but excluded by
  default because it was generated from future demand. When explicitly enabled it
  is taken from the latest snapshot on or before the decision date.

Recommended decisions use the position for reorder-point comparisons and
order-quantity targets. The position is clipped at zero for reporting.

---

## 5. Lead-time demand

Preferred calculation: **sum the daily forecast over the supplier lead time.**

```
LeadTimeDemand = Σ forecast_units[d+1 … d+L]
```

- Weekday, festival and seasonal shape in the forecast is preserved.
- Per-SKU lead times come from `sku_master.supplier_lead_time_days`. Missing,
  non-numeric or non-positive values use `inventory.default_lead_time_days`
  (default 14) and are flagged `lead_time_defaulted`.
- If the forecast horizon is shorter than the lead time, the shortfall is filled
  from the **mean of the available forecast days** and flagged
  `lead_time_exceeds_forecast_horizon`. Actual future demand is never used.
- If no forecast exists at all, the trailing-average fallback is used and flagged.

---

## 6. Safety stock

Default method: `demand_volatility`.

```
σ_daily   = std(trailing daily units, ddof=1)
σ_LTD     = σ_daily × sqrt(lead_time_days)
SafetyStock = z(target_service_level) × σ_LTD
```

where `z` is the standard-normal quantile (`0.90 → 1.2816`, `0.95 → 1.6449`,
`0.97 → 1.8808`, `0.99 → 2.3263`).

Optional methods (configurable, tested):

- `forecast_error`: uses a supplied historical forecast-error standard deviation
  per day when available.
- `empirical`: uses a historical percentile of rolling lead-time demand minus the
  mean.

Assumptions and fallbacks:

- The default formula assumes stationary daily demand over the lead time. Fashion
  jewellery has festivals, promotions and lifecycle effects, so this is an
  approximation.
- Synthetic observed sales are stockout-censored, which can **understate** true
  demand variability.
- SKUs with fewer than `min_history_days_for_volatility` observations use a
  category-level median volatility fallback and are flagged
  `short_history_category_volatility_fallback`.
- Zero-demand SKUs yield zero volatility and zero safety stock when their history
  is genuinely all zeros.
- A configured service level is a **target**, not proof that the service level is
  achieved. No measured service-level guarantee is implied.

---

## 7. Reorder point and statuses

```
ReorderPoint = LeadTimeDemand + SafetyStock
```

Status mapping:

| Status | Rule |
|---|---|
| `Critical` | Stockout risk is Critical: existing stockout or projected stockout within `risk.critical_cover_days` (default 3). |
| `At Risk` | Stockout risk is High (stock runs out before replenishment can arrive) or Medium. |
| `Reorder Required` | Inventory position is below the reorder point, but stockout risk is not High/Critical. |
| `Healthy` | Inventory position is at or above the reorder point and stockout risk is Low. |

Stockout-risk levels are defined in Section 9. All thresholds are configurable
under `inventory.risk`; they are planning rules, not statistical certainties.

---

## 8. Target stock and replenishment quantity

```
ProtectionPeriod = LeadTime + ReviewPeriod          (if protection_period_includes_review)
TargetStock      = ExpectedDemandDuringProtectionPeriod + SafetyStock
OrderQuantity    = max(0, TargetStock − InventoryPosition)
```

Optional constraints, applied only when configured:

- `replenishment.min_order_qty` (default 0: no MOQ assumption),
- `replenishment.order_multiple` (default 1: no pack-size assumption),
- `replenishment.max_storage_units` (default null: no capacity assumption).

The synthetic dataset has **no supplier MOQ, pack-size, capacity or purchase-cost
information**, so no such rules are invented. The resulting order quantity is always
a non-negative integer. Constraint adjustments are flagged
(`raised_to_min_order_qty`, `rounded_to_order_multiple`, `capped_by_max_storage`).

---

## 9. Stockout risk

Rules use projected stock cover. Days of cover uses on-hand stock; projected
stockout dates also account for scheduled incoming orders.

- `days_of_cover = on_hand / expected_daily_demand` (NaN when expected demand is 0).
- `projected_stockout_date` = first future day where forecast demand exceeds
  on-hand stock plus arrivals scheduled on or before that day.
- Levels:
  - **Critical:** existing stockout, or projected stockout within
    `critical_cover_days`.
  - **High:** projected stockout before the supplier lead time elapses.
  - **Medium:** projected stockout before lead time + review period (when
    `medium_cover_includes_review` is true).
  - **Low:** no stockout within those windows, or no demand.

The engine distinguishes:

- **existing stockout** (`existing_stockout = True`),
- **projected stockout** (`projected_stockout_date` is set),
- **low-stock warning** (`High`/`Medium` without an existing stockout).

These are model-based warnings, not certainty.

---

## 10. Overstock detection

```
expected_demand_cover_period = expected_daily_demand × cover_threshold_days
excess_units = max(0, inventory_position − expected_demand_cover_period)
```

- Default cover threshold: 90 days (`inventory.overstock.cover_threshold_days`).
- Slow-moving and inactive flags are added when expected daily demand is at or
  below `slow_moving_daily_units` or recent sales are zero.
- If expected daily demand is zero, all positive position is excess.
- **No financial value is computed.** There is no purchase cost in the dataset, and
  selling price is not used as a substitute for cost. Excess is reported in units
  only. No holding cost is invented.

---

## 11. Historical inventory simulation

The simulation (`src/inventory/simulation.py`, `scripts/simulate_inventory.py`)
evaluates a replenishment policy over a historical window.

At each simulated day:

1. Receive purchase orders whose expected arrival date is today.
2. On review dates (`decision_frequency_days`), build a forecast whose origin is
   **yesterday**.
3. Read start-of-day on-hand stock and open orders.
4. Calculate inventory position, lead-time demand, safety stock, reorder point,
   target stock and recommended order quantity.
5. Place a simulated purchase order if the engine recommends one.
6. Serve that day's observed demand proxy from stock.
7. Record lost sales, closing stock and inventory position.
8. Continue to the next day.

### Controlled comparison

Every policy uses the same:

- simulation start and end dates,
- starting on-hand stock (start-of-day stock at the start date),
- demand series,
- supplier lead times,
- review frequency and service-level configuration.

By default every policy starts with an **empty pipeline**
(`include_opening_purchase_orders: false`). Opening historical purchase orders are
not inherited because they were produced by the generator's original policy, and
inheriting them would confound the comparison.

### Implemented policies

1. **`forecast_driven`** — uses the causal M3 model forecast (frozen or rolling).
2. **`simple_rop`** — uses a causal trailing-mean forecast baseline.

Historical replenishment behaviour is **not** reconstructed as a third policy:
the generator did not store true latent demand or the complete historical decision
state, so a fair reconstruction is not possible. This limitation is stated in every
report.

### Demand and lost sales

The generator stores **stockout-censored observed sales**, not latent customer
demand. The simulation uses observed `units_sold` as a common **demand proxy** and
reports unfulfilled units as **"lost sales proxy units"** for that proxy. It does
**not** claim to reconstruct true lost demand. Because every policy sees the same proxy, relative
policy comparisons remain internally consistent, while absolute fill rates may be
optimistic. The generator does not persist latent demand; if it did, it must be kept
separate from model inputs.

### No look-ahead

- Forecast origins are always `decision_date − 1 day`.
- The frozen provider fits once at the first origin; the rolling provider refits
  only at the then-current origin.
- Feature engineering uses backward-looking lags and rolling windows only.
- Future prices/promotions are held at observed or documented defaults.
- Tests assert that every requested forecast origin is strictly before its decision
  date.

## 12. Evaluation metrics

Implemented in `src/inventory/evaluation.py`:

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Service level (SKU-day cycle service)** | Share of simulated SKU-days where all demand-proxy units were fulfilled. |
| **Fill rate** | Fulfilled units / demand-proxy units. |
| **Stockout frequency** | Stockout SKU-days / all simulated SKU-days. |
| **Lost sales proxy** | Unfulfilled demand-proxy units (not latent demand). |
| **Average inventory** | Mean start-of-day on-hand units per SKU-day. |
| **Average portfolio inventory** | Mean total on-hand units across all SKUs per day (= SKU-day average × SKU count). |
| **Inventory turnover (units-based)** | Total demand-proxy units / average portfolio inventory units over the window. This is a *period* turnover, **not** a financial/COGS turnover, because no validated product-cost data exists. |
| **Annualised turnover** | Turnover × 365 / simulation period days. |
| **Days of inventory** | Simulation period days / period turnover, exactly consistent with the annualised turnover. |

Turnover is reported in **units**, not money. Both numerator and denominator use
the same simulation window, so the result is comparable across policies. Because
per-SKU turnover distributions are skewed, the overall figure is the aggregate
ratio of totals (not the unweighted mean of per-SKU ratios).
| **Excess inventory** | Average units above the forecast-driven target stock across review decisions. |
| **Replenishment count / units** | Number and units of simulated orders. |
| **Holding cost** | **Not calculated** — no validated holding-cost input exists. |

The reports explain the trade-off: a policy that improves fill rate may also hold
more average inventory and place more orders.

## 13. Scenario analysis

`src/inventory/scenarios.py` reruns the same recommendation engine with **one**
changed assumption at a time, holding stock, forecast and other settings fixed:

- demand +10%, +20%, −10%,
- supplier lead time +7 days,
- target service level 97% / 99%.

Scenarios report changes in lead-time demand, safety stock, reorder point,
recommended order quantity, reorder/critical/overstock counts and excess units.
They are explicitly labelled **model-based scenarios**, not causal effects or
guaranteed outcomes. Promotion scenarios are not modelled separately because no
validated promotion-uplift response function is available; a demand multiplier is
the documented proxy.

## 15. Explainability

Every recommendation carries an `explanation` string, for example:

> Reorder Required: inventory position 19 is below the reorder point 32. Expected
> lead-time demand is 24.0 units, safety stock is 8.0 units, and current stock is
> 19 with 0 incoming. Recommended order quantity is 40 units to reach target stock
> 59. Stockout risk is High with 3.8 days of on-hand cover against a 6-day supplier
> lead time. Assumptions/fallbacks: lead_time_defaulted.

The explanation states why a reorder is recommended, why a SKU is at stockout
risk, why it is classified as overstocked (via quantities and cover), and which
fallbacks were applied.

## 16. Configuration

Configuration lives under `inventory:` in `config.yaml` and is merged with
documented defaults in `src/inventory/settings.py`:

- target and supported service levels,
- safety-stock method and history window,
- review period and default lead time,
- forecast horizon,
- stockout-risk thresholds,
- overstock cover/slow-moving thresholds,
- MOQ, order multiple and storage capacity,
- scenario definitions,
- simulation start/end, review frequency, forecast mode, refit interval and
  opening-purchase-order policy,
- reporting output directory and top-N rows.

No machine-specific paths or hidden supplier rules are hardcoded.

## 17. How to run

```powershell
# 1. SKU-level recommendations + scenario analysis for one decision date
python scripts/run_inventory_optimization.py --mode synthetic

# Optional: use an existing M3 model artefact (must predate the forecast origin)
python scripts/run_inventory_optimization.py --model-dir models/forecasting/synthetic/selected_model

# 2. Historical simulation comparing replenishment policies
python scripts/simulate_inventory.py --mode synthetic

# Optional: rolling refits every 30 days
python scripts/simulate_inventory.py --forecast-mode rolling --refit-every-days 30

# 3. Recompute evaluation metrics from the persisted simulation
python scripts/evaluate_inventory.py

# Tests
python -m pytest tests -q
```

Useful bounds for a quick run:

```powershell
python scripts/run_inventory_optimization.py --sku-limit 50 --no-scenarios
python scripts/simulate_inventory.py --start 2024-10-01 --end 2024-11-30 --sku-limit 50
```

## 18. Assumptions

1. Inventory optimization is supported only for the synthetic dataset.
2. Synthetic inventory, sales and purchase orders are simulated, not observed.
3. Supplier lead times come from `sku_master`; missing values use the configured
   fallback and are flagged.
4. The weekly `committed_qty` field was derived from future demand and is excluded
   from decisions by default.
5. Safety stock uses the configured method; the configured service level is a
   planning target, not a measured guarantee.
6. Forecasts are used only from information available at the forecast origin.
7. Observed sales are a demand proxy and are stockout-censored.
8. No purchase cost, supplier MOQ, pack size, capacity or holding cost exists;
   these are not fabricated.
9. Scenario changes are model-based illustrations, not causal estimates.
10. Simulation results describe behaviour under synthetic assumptions and are not
    proof of real-world performance.

---

## 19. Limitations

- Synthetic data does not represent any real retailer, supplier or warehouse.
- Stockout-censored sales can understate true customer demand and make absolute
  fill rates optimistic.
- Latent demand is not stored by the generator, so true lost sales cannot be
  reconstructed. Historical replenishment behaviour cannot be compared fairly.
- The safety-stock formula assumes approximate stationarity and normally
  distributed lead-time demand. Intermittent and zero-demand SKUs use fallbacks.
- Forecast errors propagate into inventory decisions; a bad forecast can cause
  over-ordering or stockouts.
- Lead times are deterministic in the simulation. Supplier unreliability, partial
  deliveries, MOQ rules and capacity constraints are not modelled unless explicitly
  configured.
- No monetary cost or savings are computed or claimed.
- UCI and M5 have no inventory records and must not be used for inventory
  optimization.
